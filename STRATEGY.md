# AEGIS — THE TEN YEAR PLAN

> Written 2026-08-27, after 1,021 specifications tested and zero promoted.
> This document is deliberately unflattering. A plan that only works if the research succeeds is not a plan.

---

## 0. THE POSITION, STATED HONESTLY

**What exists.** 1,021 specifications tested across 32 families. 2.27M counted trials. Sixteen machine guards, each
verified to FAIL before its green was trusted. Fourteen laws, every one derived from a measured error rather than
imported from a textbook. An immutable pre-registration table. Six forward clocks, scored daily. A complete,
queryable, adversarial record of what does not work.

**What does not exist.** A tradable edge. Not one specification has been promoted. Zero dollars have been risked and
zero dollars earned.

**What today demonstrated.** A settlement-failure signal reached t −8.84 and survived ten consecutive attacks —
liquidity, short interest, publication lag, era stability, threshold, horizon, momentum, survivorship, volume,
independence. It would have made a spectacular pitch deck. The eleventh test (D-627) showed the short leg **rises**
and loses money standalone; the real excess is roughly a quarter of the headline, reachable only by shorting the
hardest-to-borrow names in the market.

**That eleventh test is the product.** Not the signal. The capacity to kill a beautiful number on the day it is born.

## 1. THE REFRAME: WHY THIS HASN'T BEEN DONE IS WHY IT MUST BE

Every reason this does not already exist is a structural incentive, not a technical barrier. Structural gaps are the
only durable moats, because they persist even after everyone can see them.

| Why nobody has built it | Why that is precisely the opening |
|---|---|
| **No one is paid to publish negative results in finance.** Journals want findings; funds want assets; educators want subscribers. | The negative-result corpus is therefore *unowned*. 1,021 verdicts nobody else has any reason to produce is an asset that compounds and cannot be cheaply copied. |
| **The industry sells hope.** A product that says "this does not work" destroys the thing being sold. | Hope is abundant and worthless. Verified disbelief is scarce and priceable. The buyer is anyone whose losses come from acting on unfalsified claims — which is nearly everyone. |
| **Falsification has no distribution channel.** There is no bestseller called *Nothing Works*. | Distribution follows trust, and trust follows being right about failure. Every retracted claim in this ledger is a credential no marketing budget can buy. |
| **Career incentives punish "we found nothing."** | We have no career to protect. Twelve retractions today, all self-inflicted and self-recorded, is a track record of a kind nobody else can produce because nobody else can afford to. |
| **Rigor is expensive and slow.** Sixteen guards, pre-registration, forward clocks — all cost time and find nothing. | Automated, it costs near-zero *per marginal test*. The fixed cost is already paid. That is the scale advantage: our cost of saying "no" is now lower than anyone's cost of saying "maybe". |

**The thesis in one line:** the market for *"here is what works"* is saturated, adversarial, and mostly fraudulent.
The market for *"here is what provably does not, and here is the evidence"* is empty and structurally defensible.

## 2. WHAT "MARKET DEFINER" ACTUALLY REQUIRES

A market definer is not the loudest participant. It is the reference point others must position against. Three
thresholds, in order — and none can be skipped:

1. **Reliability threshold.** The system is trusted by one operator for decisions with real consequences. If it
   cannot be relied upon by one person, "millions" is a fantasy.
2. **Defensibility threshold.** An adversarial outsider — a quant who wants us wrong — cannot find a hole we have not
   already recorded ourselves. This is the hardest and least glamorous of the three.
3. **Reference threshold.** Others cite the verdict record when justifying their own decisions. At that point the
   standard is ours, and being the standard is the business.

## 3. THE FOUR SEGMENTS

### SEGMENT I — SOVEREIGN (Year 1)
*Objective: one operator can rely on it completely.*

- Every claim in the ledger carries its coverage, breadth, liquidity, effect-size, execution and holdability statement
  or is marked UNTESTED. No exceptions, enforced by guard.
- All six forward clocks scored automatically; a matured clock without a verdict turns the board red.
- Operator-facing surfaces: one command shows guard state, one shows the paper rung, one shows the verdict record.
  No component requires me to invoke it.
- Paid-data decisions made deliberately: options surface, intraday equities, TRACE. Each with a stated hypothesis it
  would test and a kill condition, purchased only against that.
- **Exit criterion:** thirty consecutive days where the daily runner completes, all guards green, and no manual
  intervention is required.

### SEGMENT II — DEFENSIBLE (Years 2–3)
*Objective: survives hostile external scrutiny.*

- Independent replication: the entire ledger reproducible from raw data by a third party with our scripts and no
  contact with us. Anything not reproducible is deleted, not explained.
- Adversarial review commissioned deliberately — pay a quant to break it, publish what they break.
- The forward clocks mature. Most will fail; D-608 and D-615 are both predicted to. **Publishing those failures on
  schedule, having predicted them in advance, is the single highest-value credential this project can earn.**
- Data moat: every free dataset in the accessible universe ingested, not the convenient subset. Today found three
  coverage gaps that had silently distorted conclusions.
- **Exit criterion:** an outside expert attempts refutation and finds nothing we have not already recorded against
  ourselves.

### SEGMENT III — DISTRIBUTED (Years 4–6)
*Objective: others depend on it.*

- The verdict database as a queryable product. Not signals — *refutations*, with full provenance.
- The falsification engine as a service: submit a strategy, receive a pre-registered adversarial test and a verdict
  you cannot negotiate with.
- First institutional buyers are allocators and prop firms, whose expensive problem is **rejecting** strategies, not
  finding them. Our cost per rejection is near-zero; theirs is a funded allocation that fails.
- Public corpus: the negative-result record published continuously, which is simultaneously the product, the
  marketing, and the moat.
- **Exit criterion:** external parties cite our verdicts in their own decisions without being asked.

### SEGMENT IV — DEFINING (Years 7–10)
*Objective: the standard others are measured against.*

- Pre-registration and adversarial verdicts become an expected disclosure in the segments we touch — the way
  audited accounts are expected of a public company.
- The laws generalise beyond trading. Coverage, Breadth, Effect-Size, Execution, Selection, Sign, Instrument,
  Benchmark, Pre-Commitment, Mechanism — none are finance-specific. Every field with a replication problem needs them.
- Capital deployment **only if** something clears the gates, and at a size the evidence supports. If nothing ever
  clears, the business stands on the engine alone. **That is the design, not the fallback.**
- **Exit criterion:** the counterfactual question "was this pre-registered?" is asked by default in our domain.

## 4. HOW IT MAKES MONEY — WITHOUT ASSUMING AN EDGE

I will not promise trading returns. Nothing here has cleared the gates, and today's best candidate shrank by 75% on
its eleventh test. A plan resting on future alpha is a wish.

Four revenue paths, ordered by how little they assume:

1. **Rejection-as-a-service (assumes nothing).** Allocators lose millions funding strategies that were never tested
   adversarially. A verdict costs us near-zero and saves them a full allocation. This works even if we never find a
   single edge.
2. **The verdict corpus (assumes only accumulation).** 1,021 specifications and growing, each with provenance and a
   kill condition. Subscription access. Value grows monotonically with time and cannot be back-filled by a competitor
   starting later.
3. **The engine, licensed (assumes the method generalises).** Pre-registration, immutable rules, machine guards,
   forward scoring. Any research organisation with a replication problem needs this substrate.
4. **Managed capital (assumes an edge exists).** Last, smallest, and gated behind the ladder. If nothing clears,
   this path stays closed permanently — and the first three still stand.

**The asymmetry that makes this fundable:** paths 1–3 do not require us to be right about markets. They require us to
be right about *rigor*, and that is already demonstrated by a ledger of self-inflicted retractions.

## 5. THE MONTH — 2026-08-27 to 2026-09-27

Concrete, verifiable, and it must survive my absence.

**Week 1 — Close the record.**
- Every unresolved item in `trd_lineage` gets a coverage statement or an UNTESTED verdict.
- Reproduce three ledger claims chosen at random from raw data. Anything that fails to reproduce is retracted, not
  explained. (D-581's 8% drift is a live example of why this matters.)
- Guard suite runs thirty days unattended. Any silent failure is a Segment-I blocker.

**Week 2 — Close the coverage gaps.**
- Enumerate every free dataset in the accessible universe against what we hold. Today found three gaps that had
  already distorted conclusions; assume more exist.
- FRED and TRACE: operator-gated, so the deliverable is the ingest built and waiting on a key, not a request.
- Every gap either filled or recorded with the verdict it currently blocks.

**Week 3 — Make the forward clocks unignorable.**
- Each of the six clocks gets a spec-specific scoring script, so maturity produces a *number* rather than a flag.
- Written predictions for each, made now: D-608's clock is predicted to FAIL (decay measured at D-611); D-615's is
  predicted to fail on the borrow constraint (D-627).
- Predictions recorded in `trd_prereg` where they cannot be edited when the data arrives.

**Week 4 — Build the first external surface.**
- One page: what has been tested, what died, why, and what it cost to find out.
- Not a pitch. A record. The audience is the person who will check whether we retract our own claims.
- **Exit criterion for the month:** an outsider can read the record, reproduce a claim, and find a retraction we made
  against ourselves before they could.

## 6. THE HONEST RISKS

- **Nothing may ever clear the gates.** The programme is designed so this is survivable — paths 1–3 do not need it.
  It would still be a disappointment, and it should be said plainly rather than hedged.
- **The engine's value depends on the ledger being trustworthy.** One unretracted false claim costs more than a
  hundred honest nulls. This is why the guards inspect running code and not only conclusions.
- **I am unreliable at mechanisms.** Today: seven mechanism stories proposed, seven dead, one reported as a win
  before being retracted. The machinery exists because of this, not despite it.
- **Rigor does not scale by willpower.** Every control that worked today was mechanical. Every failure was a moment
  where judgement substituted for a check.
