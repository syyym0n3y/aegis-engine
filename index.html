What we found

  • Seven edges survive the full gate — across 170+ instruments and 7 timeframes (5m→weekly), judged per-instrument vs its own random entry, deflated, and stress-tested for survivorship: rip-short (equity daily short), bbfade_lo/bear (equity daily long in down-markets), crypto momentum (Donchian breakout), the variance risk premium (option-selling — implied vol beats realized 80–87% of the time; CBOE PutWrite/BuyWrite beat SPY risk-adjusted over 34 years), pairs / stat-arb (market-neutral spread reversion), the commodity term-structure roll premium (long backwardation / short contango — front-month rolling bleeds 7–13%/yr), and cross-sectional momentum (the classic 12-1m relative-strength factor). The pattern: documented risk premia plus a few regime-conditioned technical patterns survive — every piece of undocumented folklore (ICT/liquidity, VWAP fades, chart seasonality, intermarket lead-lag, FX carry, equity breakouts) was rejected.
  • The definitive global verdict (full coverage): we swept 100% of the US universe (9,850 SEC filers) and 693 FX-normalized international names across 18 exchanges, with realistic per-tier spread + borrow. rip-short is US-quality-large-cap-specific — strong on the curated liquid set (t=6.77) but negative on US mid/small/micro and flat-to-negative across every international tier. It is likely a US-microstructure effect (borrow depth, options-hedging flow) that does not replicate abroad. The broad risk-premia edges (VRP, pairs, term-structure, momentum) are global; the technical patterns are narrow. The illiquid ~50,000-name global tail holds no edge.
  • Judge each instrument on its OWN terms — not the market's regime, not a pooled average. Pooling can both manufacture and mask real structure; a market-wide (SPY) regime imposed on gold/FX/crypto is a bias. Count how many instruments individually beat their own random entry (binomial null).
  • A 1R-capped stop is structurally survivorship-proof, in any direction — a name going to zero contributes bounded −1R stop-outs, never catastrophic loss, so the missing dead names can't inflate the edge. That's why crypto momentum holds even on coins that cratered 54–100%.
  • Crypto is a momentum market; equities are mean-reversion — opposite structures. Match the setup family to the asset's character; the old "mean-reversion beats momentum" rule was equity-specific.
  • The cost wall kills profit, not skill — mean-reversion beats random even at 5m, but the spread makes it net-negative. Edges are daily-locked because only slow bars let skill clear the cost (cost-in-R: 5m 0.31 → daily 0.03).
  • Beating a RANDOM entry is everything — positive expectancy is worthless (a random long earns +0.15–0.25R on drift alone). A setup is real only if it beats a matched random entry in the same regime, deflated for how many things were tried.
