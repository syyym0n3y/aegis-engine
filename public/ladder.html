§1

The finding that reorganises everything else

Sorted by family, this corpus looks like broad coverage. Sorted by capital, it looks like something else entirely.

Two-thirds of the work cannot be held at the rung being climbed

681 of 1,059 specifications (64.3%) are long-short constructions — quintile spreads, decile sorts, WML, HML, pair trades. Every one requires margin and stock borrow. Neither exists in an account below roughly $10,000. That is not an emphasis problem; it is two-thirds of the research being structurally inapplicable to the account it was meant to grow.

The comfortable explanation would be that the long-only region is simply unexplored. It isn't. 267 long-only-viable specifications have been tested, and their best portfolio t is 1.58 against a deflation ceiling of 5.4555. The imbalance is a misallocation of effort, not an unexplored seam.

§2

Levels are set by cost regime, not round numbers — the boundary is where fixed cost stops exceeding any plausible edge. Every figure below is walked on real market history from $40 at $100/mo, with paths that ran out of panel counted as censored rather than as failures.

L0$40 – $1,000
Contribution phase. Reached in 733 of 733 paths, median 0.8 years — of which 94% is money contributed. Static and commissioned, $40 compounds at −6.4%/yr and never arrives. solved by deposits

Walked on 63 years of total-return market history (1963-07 to 2026-06, 756 months, 733 start months), not a simulated distribution — the sequence is the point. Starting in 1999 puts the dot-com bust where the account is smallest; starting in 2009 buys a decade of tailwind first. Worst drawdown along the way is a median −46.9% and the longest stretch under water is 6.1 years. Commission barely registers: the pre-2019 $5-per-trade regime costs about half a year at L3.

What an edge is worth, priced in deposits

Median years to $100,000: 20.8 at $100/mo with no alpha. A sustained +5%/yr net alpha — which this programme has never produced — brings it to 16.7. Simply depositing $150/mo instead of $100 brings it to 17.4.

The alpha buys 4.1 years. The deposit buys 3.4, is available this afternoon, and is certain.

§5

What would change the answer

You cannot buy your way out of the deposit-dominated phase

At the median path to $1,000, $940 of the $1,000 is money contributed. At $10,000 it is still 69%; by $100,000 the ratio has inverted and return supplies 75%.

The first version of this page called the turn a $24,114 threshold. It isn't one. Swept across a 20× range of deposit rates, the crossover lands at 9.8 years every single time — only the account size moves, exactly in proportion. Both contributions and account value are linear in the monthly amount, so their ratio is deposit-free.

Starting capital does move it: $5,000 up front cuts it to 7.4 years, $25,000 to 6.3. That asymmetry is the check — had the starting balance done nothing either, the metric would have been broken rather than invariant, and the sweep would have looked identical from outside.

There is a fifth lever nobody had examined: which passive holding. Every result above assumed the US total market as though it were the only zero-borrow, $1-floor option. Eight fixed-weight ETF baskets, monthly rebalanced, over 21.8 years containing 2008 and 2020:

Five of seven higher-Sharpe alternatives delay the ladder

The 4-way diversified basket gains +0.09 Sharpe and costs 5.8 years to the crossover, reaching $100,000 in no eligible path. A 60/40 gains 0.06 and costs 4.6 years.

An account compounding toward a level does not care about risk-adjusted return. It cares about return. Diversification that lowers the compounding rate lengthens the deposit-dominated phase — the phase that depositing harder already cannot shorten. This is the opposite of the standard recommendation, and the disagreement is not about the statistics; it is that the objective is different.

Six forward clocks are registered, with numeric two-sided promote/kill rules written into an append-only table before their data existed. Earliest maturity is roughly twelve months. Five of the six carry a written prediction that they will fail — recorded in advance, so the calibration record survives whichever way they land.

The first version of this page reported L3 as censored — every path ran out of panel before the ~25 years $100/mo compounding needs. That was true of the 305-month series it used and not of the question: a 63-year market history was already on disk. Re-walked on it, L3 has zero eligible failures. The censoring was a property of the series, not of the strategy, and the honest move was to go and get the longer series rather than keep reporting the gap.

What that leaves is a programme whose research output is not currently the constraint on its own account. For the first ~10 years the deposit rate decides, regardless of how fast it deposits; after that, nothing among 1,059 tested specifications has cleared the bar. Both halves of that sentence are measurements, and neither is an argument for doing less work — they say where the next unit of work has to land to matter.

§6

Half the losses were the fee

A flat per-period cost shifts the mean and leaves the variance alone — so charging a losing book manufactures its own significance. We checked every claim we had ever made of that shape.

The engine stores gross and net returns for each specification, so the pre-cost t-statistic is recoverable exactly. Across all 1,059: 137 claim a significant loss, and 64 of them (47%) are not significant before the assumed fee.

The largest t-statistic this programme ever recorded has the opposite sign gross

Charging the cost was never wrong — trading an intraday split really does pay a round trip a day. What was wrong is that the ledger stored the net t as the t, so every reader saw −22.30 and read a fact about markets. The honest sentence is "there is no overnight effect, and trading it would cost 50%/yr on top."

The audit discriminates rather than condemns: delisting notices survive at −3.76 gross, 23 of 35 timing claims hold, and turn-of-month holds. An audit that killed everything would be measuring its own threshold.

Fixing it exposed a second layer. The function generating every cross-sectional specification charged a full round trip every rebalance — an implied 100% turnover. Measured, the median is 17% one-way for equities and 35% for perpetuals, so the median spec was overcharged nearly 3×. Correcting it cuts both ways: 1-month reversal turns over 87% and had been undercharged. It changed no conclusion — zero specifications clear the significance bar before or after — which is the result worth reporting rather than the rescue that wasn't there.

§7

And three-quarters of the wins were the universe

A long-short spread can be large and precisely measured while both of its legs earn exactly what everything else earned. The engine computed that check and threw the answer away.

A quantile spread says nothing about whether either leg earns anything. The test that separates a signal from drift is whether the top bucket beats its own universe over the same periods — a number the engine calculated on every specification and then collapsed into a yes/no flag. Two arithmetic passes characterise the entire corpus

Most claims that something loses were about the assumed fee — 47% of significant losses. Most claims that something makes money were about the universe — 74% of positive results.

Neither required reading a single row's prose. Both were recoverable from numbers the engine had already computed and was discarding.

The 90 that survive are dominated by cash-flow and payout yields — free cash flow (excess 7.82%/yr at t 3.99), operating cash flow, buyback yield, accruals. That is the same family placed on a forward clock six days earlier, by a completely different filter. The convergence adds one narrow thing worth stating: those registered leads are not universe drift. Whatever the forward clock eventually says about them, it will not be answering this question.

None of the 90 clears the significance bar. The board is unchanged.
