§1

The finding that reorganises everything else

Sorted by family, this corpus looks like broad coverage. Sorted by capital, it looks like something else entirely.

Two-thirds of the work cannot be held at the rung being climbed

681 of 1,059 specifications (64.3%) are long-short constructions — quintile spreads, decile sorts, WML, HML, pair trades. Every one requires margin and stock borrow. Neither exists in an account below roughly $10,000. That is not an emphasis problem; it is two-thirds of the research being structurally inapplicable to the account it was meant to grow.

The comfortable explanation would be that the long-only region is simply unexplored. It isn't. 267 long-only-viable specifications have been tested, and their best portfolio t is 1.58 against a deflation ceiling of 5.4555. The imbalance is a misallocation of effort, not an unexplored seam.

§2

The ladder, and what is actually available on each rung

Levels are set by cost regime, not round numbers — the boundary is where fixed cost stops exceeding any plausible edge. Every figure below is walked on real market history from $40 at $100/mo, with paths that ran out of panel counted as censored rather than as failures.

L1 — the crossover$1,000 – $10,000
Cumulative return first exceeds cumulative contributions at $24,114, median 9.8 years. Below that line the deposit rate is the account; above it the strategy is. The line sits almost exactly where margin and borrow arrive — so the point where research starts mattering and the point where two-thirds of it becomes placeable are the same point. the boundary that matters
L0$40 – $1,000
Contribution phase. Reached in 733 of 733 paths, median 0.8 years — of which 94% is money contributed. Static and commissioned, $40 compounds at −6.4%/yr and never arrives. solved by deposits

Walked on 63 years of total-return market history (1963-07 to 2026-06, 756 months, 733 start months), not a simulated distribution — the sequence is the point. Starting in 1999 puts the dot-com bust where the account is smallest; starting in 2009 buys a decade of tailwind first. Worst drawdown along the way is a median −46.9% and the longest stretch under water is 6.1 years. Commission barely registers: the pre-2019 $5-per-trade regime costs about half a year at L3.

What an edge is worth, priced in deposits

Median years to $100,000: 20.8 at $100/mo with no alpha. A sustained +5%/yr net alpha — which this programme has never produced — brings it to 16.7. Simply depositing $150/mo instead of $100 brings it to 17.4.

The alpha buys 4.1 years. The deposit buys 3.4, is available this afternoon, and is certain.

§5

What would change the answer

The honest allocation of effort below the crossover

At the median path to $1,000, $940 of the $1,000 is money contributed. At $10,000 it is still 69%. Only past $24,114 does cumulative return overtake cumulative deposits — and by $100,000 the ratio has inverted, with return supplying 75%.

So the research programme is not the binding constraint on the account until roughly $24k. This is uncomfortable to write inside a research programme, and it is what 733 real start months say.

Six forward clocks are registered, with numeric two-sided promote/kill rules written into an append-only table before their data existed. Earliest maturity is roughly twelve months. Five of the six carry a written prediction that they will fail — recorded in advance, so the calibration record survives whichever way they land.

The first version of this page reported L3 as censored — every path ran out of panel before the ~25 years $100/mo compounding needs. That was true of the 305-month series it used and not of the question: a 63-year market history was already on disk. Re-walked on it, L3 has zero eligible failures. The censoring was a property of the series, not of the strategy, and the honest move was to go and get the longer series rather than keep reporting the gap.

What that leaves is a programme whose research output is not currently the constraint on its own account. Below $24,114 the deposit rate decides; above it, nothing among 1,059 tested specifications has cleared the bar. Both halves of that sentence are measurements, and neither is an argument for doing less work — they say where the next unit of work has to land to matter.

Figures read directly from the engine's ledger on the date stamped below. Aegis is a single-operator research programme; no signal is published, nothing is promoted to capital, and this is a synthesis of what was measured rather than advice about anyone's money.
